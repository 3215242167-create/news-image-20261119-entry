# The World Today — 19 November 2026

A one-page newspaper front page that sums up Thursday 19 November 2026,
built for make.fun's news-image-20261119 bounty
("Sum up 19 November's world news in one image").

## What it is

`main.py` draws the whole front page from scratch with Python and Pillow:
masthead, date line, six news stories and three short briefs, plus a small
line-drawn globe. The text is set with Noto Serif and Noto Sans from the
system fonts. Nothing is downloaded, nothing is generated by an image model,
and the bundled `entry.png` is exactly what `main.py` produces when run.

Stories on the page, all first reported on 19 November 2026:

- **Summit (APEC):** Trump, Xi and Putin meet in the first-ever three-way
  summit; nuclear arms control leads the agenda as the last US-Russia treaty
  runs out in February, and Ukraine is not on the agenda.
- **Gaza:** Israeli forces cross the "Yellow Line" in central Gaza in the
  first major ground operation since the October 2025 ceasefire; Hamas calls
  it a flagrant violation.
- **The Hague:** the International Court of Justice rules today on
  Gambia v. Myanmar over the Rohingya; a genocide finding would make Myanmar
  the first state held liable under the 1948 Genocide Convention.
- **Washington:** House Democrats nominate Hakeem Jeffries for Speaker; he
  is set to become the first Black Speaker.
- **Culture:** GTA VI unlocks worldwide after thirteen years; early reviews
  score it 99 on Metacritic.
- **Weather:** Cyclone Akvan nears landfall between Machilipatnam and Puri;
  Andhra Pradesh and Odisha are on high alert.
- **Also today:** a Van Gogh sells for a record $190.4 million; NASA's
  Voyager 1 reaches one light-day from Earth; Modi meets the Prince and
  Princess of Wales.

## How to run it

```sh
MAKEFUN_OUTPUT_DIR=out python3 main.py
```

That writes `out/entry.png` (1024x1024 PNG, under 1 MiB). Without
`MAKEFUN_OUTPUT_DIR` it writes to `/output`. The program needs only Python 3
and Pillow, uses no network, no random numbers and no clock, so repeated runs
produce identical bytes.
